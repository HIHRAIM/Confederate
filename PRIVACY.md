# Confederate Privacy Policy

_Last updated: 2026-08-07_

Confederate is a self-hosted, open-source relay bot that bridges Discord channels/threads and Telegram chats/topics into shared conversation spaces. This document describes what data the software processes, why, for how long, and what choices users have.

> **Who is responsible for your data.** Confederate is software, not a service: anyone can run their own instance. The person or team operating a given instance (the **operator**) controls that instance's database, configuration and backups, and is the data controller for it. This document describes what the software itself does; a specific operator may add their own infrastructure (hosting, logging, backups) around it.

## What the bot processes

To relay a message between chats, the bot receives it through the Discord and Telegram APIs, reformats it (header with the sender's name and community, mentions, attachments, forward/reply attribution) and posts copies to the other chats of the bridge. **Relaying is the product**: by design, messages written in a bridged chat become visible in every other chat of that bridge, including communities and platforms the author may not be in.

Messages are only relayed for users who have accepted the **forwarding consent** (the prompt with the "Accept" button, also available via `/verify`). Until consent is given, a user's messages are not relayed: the first message is held back (see retention below) and later ones are deleted from the bridged chat.

A bridge can also carry one more kind of message: the posts of a public Bluesky account, YouTube channel or Telegram channel that an admin attached with `/setbskyfeed` / `/setytfeed` / `/settgfeed`. Those are read from Bluesky, YouTube and Telegram and posted into the bridge's chats; nothing about the chats or their members is sent the other way (see "Where data goes").

An operator may also register **another Telegram bot** with `/setinbox`, after which the software polls that bot's private chats and turns each person writing to it into a bridge of its own — a thread and/or a topic in the chats the operator named. There the consent works differently: writing to such a bot *is* the consent, stated in the greeting it answers `/start` with, and it counts for that conversation alone (see "Where data goes").

## What the bot stores

All data lives in a local SQLite database (`bridge.db`) on the operator's machine.

| Data | Contents | Retention |
|---|---|---|
| Relay metadata | Bridge ID, origin platform/chat/message IDs, sender ID and display name, reply linkage, forward attribution (type and source name), timestamp, and the IDs of the posted copies | **30 days**, then deleted (this window powers edit/delete propagation) |
| Message content | **Not stored**, with four exceptions listed below | — |
| Pending consent | The first message of a not-yet-verified user (serialized, so it can be relayed once they accept), consent prompt IDs | Until accepted, at most **24 hours** |
| Verification records | Platform, user ID, chat where consent was accepted, timestamps | **365 days** from consent, then expired and deleted |
| Bridge rules | The rules text saved with `/remindrules` | Until `/remindrules disable` |
| Polls | Question, options, per-user votes (platform + user ID + chosen option; used to enforce one changeable vote per user — other users never see who voted for what) | Deleted **7 days** after the poll closes |
| Localization suggestions | Suggester's platform, ID and username, target language, reply code, suggested text | Until answered with `/loc-reply`, at most **1 year** |
| Admin and moderation lists | Chat admins, bridge admins, shadow-banned user IDs | Until changed/removed, or until the bot leaves the chat |
| Delegated roles | Server-wide Local Admins (`/setlocaladmin`), Localizers (`/localizer-add`) and Bridge Admins (`/setadmin`, per bridge or across a whole server/group): platform, user ID, the server/group or bridge the grant covers, username at delegation time, the delegating admin's ID, timestamp | Until revoked (`/remadmin`, `/remlocaladmin`, `/localizer-rem`) |
| Appeals | Appellant's Discord user ID, appeal thread/bridge IDs, chosen language, status, verdict timestamp and the deciding user's ID | While open; **30 days** after the verdict, or immediately when the appeal thread is deleted |
| Consul anonymization map | Appeal thread ID, consul's Discord user ID, per-thread index (which letter they appear as) | Deleted together with the appeal |
| Consul aliases | For consuls who set one with `/setname`: their Discord user ID, the chosen alias, its comparison form, who set it and when | Until changed or reset; **not** deleted with the appeal |
| Receiver bots (`/setinbox`) | The registered bot's Telegram ID, username and display name, its token **encrypted** with the operator's `BACKUP_KEY`, whether staff are anonymized, and the platform/ID of whoever registered it | Until unregistered with `/reminbox` |
| Inbox host chats (`/setinboxchat`) | Which chats a receiver bot opens conversations in, who added each and when, and whether that community hides the relay header (`/close-header`) | Until removed with `/reminboxchat`, or with the bot |
| Inbox conversations | The writer's Telegram user ID, the receiver bot's ID, the conversation's bridge ID, the writer's client language, their display name (the thread and topic are named after it), which side spoke last, and the timestamps of opening and of the last message | While open; closed and deleted after **30 days** of silence, on `/close`, on a ban, or when the bot is unregistered |
| Inbox topic map | Which Telegram topic of a host group belongs to which writer, so a closed conversation reopens in the same topic instead of a new one | Kept **across closings**; deleted when the host chat or the receiver bot is removed |
| Inbox staff anonymization map | Conversation bridge ID, staff member's platform and user ID, per-conversation index (which letter they appear as) | Deleted together with the conversation |
| Inbox bans (`/inboxban`) | Receiver bot ID, banned user's Telegram ID, who banned them and when | Until lifted with `/inboxunban`, or until the bot is unregistered |
| Chat settings | Language (`/lang`, `/locallang`), `/allow-bots`, `/webhooks`, deadchat/deadtopic/newschat configuration | Until changed/removed, or until the bot leaves the chat |
| File re-upload consent (`/allow-files`) | Which servers/groups and which bridges consented to re-uploading Telegram files, who enabled it and when | Until turned off with `/allow-files disable` |
| Re-uploaded Telegram files | Where `/allow-files` is enabled: **the files themselves**, copied to a Discord channel (`GALLERY`), plus their CDN links, the source Telegram message IDs and the upload timestamp — see "Where data goes" | **Kept indefinitely**, so that the links handed out in the copies keep working; removed when the message's deletion is proxied, or replaced when an edit changes the attachments |
| Privacy switches (`/privacy`) | Platform, user ID and the three switches (hide from `/whois`, hide avatar in webhook copies, refuse `/mention`), plus the time they were last changed. A row exists only for users who opened the command | Until the user changes them |
| Followed sources (`/setbskyfeed`, `/setytfeed`, `/settgfeed`) | The kind (Bluesky, YouTube or Telegram), the handle and display name, the chat it was attached in, the channel's numeric ID where Telegram gives one, the ID of the last relayed post, the admin who attached it and when | Until detached with `/rembskyfeed` / `/remytfeed` / `/remtgfeed`, or until the bot leaves the chat's server/group |
| Webhook-relay scopes (`/webhooks`) | Which Discord servers, and which of their bridges, show relayed copies as webhook messages, who turned it on and when | Until turned off, or until the bot leaves the server |
| Setup deadline | For servers and groups added after the rule came into force: the platform, the server/group ID, when the bot joined and — once it has been set up — when that was noticed. No user is named | Until the bot leaves the community |

The four content exceptions are: pending first messages (≤ 24 h), bridge rules, poll questions/options (≤ 7 days after close), and localization suggestions (≤ 1 year). Everything else content-wise exists only as ordinary messages inside your Discord/Telegram chats, not in the bot's database.

## Where data goes

- **Discord and Telegram.** Relayed copies are posted through the official APIs of both platforms and are subject to their own privacy policies.
- **Cross-bot verification sync (optional).** When the operator runs Confederate together with [Confederate Guard](https://github.com/HIHRAIM/Confederate-Guard), the **Discord user IDs** of users who accept or revoke the forwarding consent are posted to the configured `VERIFIED`/`UNVERIFIED` Discord channels, where Confederate Guard mirrors them into its cross-server verified database. A `VERIFIED` line also carries the ID of the server the consent was accepted on, which is what tells Guard's notice on that server apart from the ones it posts on the user's other servers. Only these Discord IDs are published — never message content, and never Telegram IDs. Operators can turn this off at runtime with `/verify-list disable`. The `/appeal` consent is an exception: it is **not** published to these channels.
- **Appeal pardon sync (optional).** When a consul grants an appeal, the appellant's **Discord user ID** is posted to the configured `APPEAL_PARDON_CHANNELS` channel, where Confederate Guard lifts the user's bans. Again, only the bare Discord ID is published.
- **Appeal ban-summary sync (optional).** When an appeal is filed, the appellant's **Discord user ID and the appeal thread ID** are posted to the configured `APPEAL_BANINFO_CHANNELS` channel. Confederate Guard answers in the appeal thread with the user's ban summary (servers, dates, reasons, issuing moderators where known); that message is pinned in the thread — visible to the appeal server's staff — and is **not** relayed to the appellant's DM.
- **Telegram file re-upload (off by default, opt-in per server/group or per bridge).** Where `/allow-files` is enabled, files sent on Telegram — documents, photos, videos, audio, GIFs — are downloaded by the bot and **re-uploaded to a Discord channel** configured by the operator (`GALLERY`), and the resulting Discord CDN links are posted in the relayed copies. Read this plainly:
  - This applies to files from **private Telegram groups** just as much as public ones. A file that only existed inside a closed group ends up stored on Discord's servers.
  - **The CDN link works for anyone who has it.** It does not check Discord accounts, server membership or channel permissions — whoever the link reaches can open the file, regardless of who may read the `GALLERY` channel itself. A link forwarded, quoted or scraped out of a relayed copy keeps working.
  - **The copy is kept indefinitely.** The bot does not delete a re-upload because of its age: a link that has been handed out to other chats keeps working, and so does the file behind it. Editing the original re-uploads the files; deleting the original removes the re-upload wherever the deletion reaches the bot. Ask the operator if you need a re-upload removed after that.
  - This is exactly why the mechanic is off by default and gated behind an explicit consent that an admin has to give: `/allow-files` for a whole server/group, or `/allow-files local` for one bridge. A bridge re-uploads only when **every** chat in it is covered — one uncovered chat and the whole bridge falls back to the marker/link representation, in which the files never leave Telegram. Stickers, voice messages and video notes are never re-uploaded.
- **Webhook avatars.** With `/webhooks` enabled, a Discord sender's own avatar is used for their copies unless they hid it with `/privacy`. Telegram senders — and Discord senders who hid theirs — get one of the bot's bundled neutral pictures, picked by the last digit of their user ID; no profile photo is downloaded or re-hosted for that.
- **Inbox conversations (opt-in, per receiver bot).** Where an operator has registered another Telegram bot with `/setinbox`, everything you send to **that bot's private chat** is forwarded into the chats its owners named — a thread on Discord, a topic on Telegram, or both at once — together with your display name, your Telegram username and your numeric Telegram ID. Their answers come back to you in the same private chat. Writing to such a bot is what constitutes the consent; its `/start` message says so before you send anything else. Nothing you write there is relayed to any other bridge, published to the Confederate Guard sync channels, or used to verify you anywhere else in the bot. The receiver bot's token is stored encrypted, never in clear.

  **Files you attach are covered by the same re-upload mechanic as everywhere else, and by its warnings.** Where the chats receiving the conversation have `/allow-files` enabled, your attachments are downloaded and re-uploaded to the operator's `GALLERY` Discord channel, and the links are posted in the copies — links that work for anyone holding them and are kept indefinitely, exactly as described above. The consent that governs this is the **receiving community's**, not yours: your private chat with the bot belongs to no community and cannot carry one. With `/allow-files` off, your files never leave Telegram and arrive as a `[N files from Telegram]` marker instead. If you would rather a file did not end up on a Discord CDN, do not send it to a support inbox.
- **Followed sources (`/setbskyfeed`, `/setytfeed`, `/settgfeed`, bridge admins only).** For every attached Bluesky account the bot periodically requests that account's public posts from Bluesky's public AppView (`public.api.bsky.app`), and — only for accounts that post video — looks the account's own server up once through its DID document (`plc.directory`, or the `did:web` host named in the handle). For every attached YouTube channel it requests the channel's public Atom feed at `youtube.com/feeds/videos.xml`, reading the channel page once to resolve a handle into a channel id; nothing but the title and the watch link is taken from it, so no YouTube file is downloaded or re-hosted. For every attached Telegram channel it either receives the posts through the Bot API (when it is one of the channel's administrators) or reads the channel's public web preview at `t.me/s/<name>`. No API key, no developer account and no third-party service is involved, and the only hosts contacted are Bluesky's, YouTube's and Telegram's. The requests carry nothing about your community: not its name, not its chats, not its members. What comes back — the posts, their links and their attachments — is relayed into the bridge, and the attachments are re-uploaded to `GALLERY` under the same "the link works for anyone who has it" caveat as above. Those hosts see the bot host's IP address making the request, exactly as they would for any visitor of the page.
- **Backups.** Every 12 hours (and on `/backup`) the database is sent to the operator-configured backup channels — **always encrypted** (authenticated BLAKE2 keystream; the key never leaves the `BACKUP_KEY` environment variable on the operator's machine). The destination channels only ever store ciphertext.
- **Service notices.** Start/stop events, the communities the bot is added to and the ones it leaves because nobody attached them to a bridge within seven days, and daily health-check findings (unreachable chats, missing permissions) go to operator-configured service channels. They contain chat names and IDs, not user data.
- **Control panel (optional).** Operators may run [Confederate Panel](https://github.com/HIHRAIM/Confederate-Panel), a self-hosted web app that reads and edits this database directly. It uses the admin/localizer delegation records above to authenticate its users (a one-time code is sent to them as a DM/private message), and posts a notice to an operator-configured log channel/topic when someone edits the bot's localization through it. Nothing is sent to third parties.

The software contains **no analytics, tracking, advertising or data-sale pipelines**, and sends nothing to the developers of Confederate.

## What other users can see

- Relayed copies show your **display name** and your community's name in the header (or as the webhook name/avatar where `/webhooks` is enabled).
- Where `/allow-files` is enabled, the files you attach are re-uploaded to Discord and shared as links that work for anyone holding them — not just the members of the bridged chats. Ask your chat's admins whether it is on if you are unsure.
- Verified users can use `/whois` on a relayed copy to see the original sender's platform, username, ID and — on Discord — online status. `/whois` is rate-limited and available only to verified users and bot admins. **You can switch this off for yourself with `/privacy`**, after which `/whois` shows no more than your nickname and avatar.
- `/mention` lets users ping a Discord user by ID/username in another bridged community (at most once per hour per target). **`/privacy` lets you refuse those pings entirely.**
- **If you write to a receiver bot:** the staff reading you can use `/whois` on your messages exactly as in any bridge, and see what your Telegram profile shows — name, username, ID, bio (unless you hid it with `/privacy`). It does not work the other way: `/whois` refuses to answer about staff inside a conversation, and you have no commands there in any case. Depending on the bot's settings, staff may reach you under their own names or anonymized as “Staff A”, “Staff B”, ….
- **If you are a consul:** by default an appellant sees you as “Consul A”, “Consul B”, … — a signature assigned per appeal thread (always in English, whatever language the appellant reads the bridge in), so the same consul appears under unrelated letters in different appeals and cannot be followed from one to the next. Setting an alias with `/setname` deliberately gives that up: the alias is one fixed string shown to **every** appellant in **every** appeal, so anyone who sees it in two appeals — or compares notes with another appellant — can tell it is the same person, and can build a history of your decisions across cases. Your real Discord identity is still not disclosed, but you are no longer unlinkable between appeals. The alias is optional and can be cleared at any time with `/setname` without a name; messages already delivered keep the signature they were sent with.

## Your choices

- **Consent is opt-in.** Nothing you write is relayed until you accept the forwarding consent; declining simply means your messages stay local (and are removed from the bridged chat). Sending `/appeal` counts as consent for the appeal bridge: your DMs to the bot are forwarded to the appeal thread, and messages written there reach you anonymized as “Consul A/B/…”. Writing to a receiver bot's private chat likewise counts as consent for that conversation alone — and only for it: it verifies you nowhere else, and stopping is a matter of not writing again.
- **Revoking consent.** Send `/unverify` at any time; your future messages will no longer be relayed until you verify again.
- **Privacy switches.** `/privacy` opens a personal menu — on either platform — with three switches you control yourself: hide yourself from `/whois` (it may then show no more than your nickname and avatar), hide your avatar in webhook copies (a neutral picture is used instead), and refuse `/mention` pings from other communities. Each switch holds in every chat of every bridge and stays on until you turn it off. Your display name in relayed copies is not covered: it is what makes a bridged conversation readable.
- **Editing and deleting.** Editing or deleting your message within 30 days updates or deletes all relayed copies as well. After 30 days the linkage metadata is gone and copies must be removed manually by chat moderators.
- **Questions and erasure requests** (e.g. removal of verification records or relayed copies older than 30 days) should go to the operator of your instance — they hold the database. For questions about the software itself, open an issue in the repository.

## Security

The database is a local file readable only by the operator's environment; backups leave the machine only encrypted; this bot's own tokens and the backup key are read from environment variables / a local `.env` file and are never written to the database or sent anywhere. The tokens of registered receiver bots are the one secret the database does hold, and they are stored encrypted under the backup key in the same authenticated format the backups use — never logged, echoed to a command or included in a reply.

## Age requirements

Confederate runs on top of Discord and Telegram and inherits their minimum-age requirements; it performs no age verification of its own and is not directed at children.
